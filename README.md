Research Methods:
This study uses electroencephalography (EEG) technology to collect brain electrical signals from participants while they make decisions. The specific methods are as follows:

Experimental Design:

Experimental Scenario: Participants were required to make decisions in two scenarios: "gain" and "loss" scenarios. In the "gain" scenario, participants faced potential positive rewards, while in the "loss" scenario, participants faced potential negative losses.

Task Type: A series of decision-making tasks were used, including investment decisions and risk choices, involving potential losses or gains. Participants needed to choose to accept or avoid based on their personal judgment.

EEG Data Acquisition:

Equipment: A high-density EEG system (such as a 64 or 128-channel EEG cap) was used for data acquisition.

Data Processing: Standard EEG preprocessing methods were used, including artifact removal (e.g., ocular artifact removal), bandpass filtering (e.g., 5-50Hz bandpass filtering), time-domain and frequency-domain analysis, etc.

Analysis Methods:

Event-Related Potential (ERP) Analysis: The focus was on ERP waveforms related to decision-making, particularly the changes in P300 and N400 waveforms associated with loss and gain responses.

Spectral Analysis: Power changes in different frequency bands (e.g., alpha waves, beta waves, theta waves) were analyzed to observe the characteristics of brain activity in loss aversion situations.

Source Localization Analysis: Brain source localization techniques (such as Laplacian or LORETA) were used to identify brain regions associated with loss aversion.

Statistical Analysis:

The brain electrical responses in the loss and gain scenarios were compared using t-tests or analysis of variance (ANOVA) to perform statistical tests of inter-group or intra-group differences.

Correlation analysis methods were used to explore the relationship between EEG signals and behavioral data (such as risk choice preferences, decision time, etc.).

Data Description

The data used in this study includes EEG signals and related behavioral data generated by participants during the decision-making tasks. The specific data description is as follows:

EEG Data:

Data Format: EEG signals are continuous time-series data with a sampling rate of 500Hz or 1000Hz. The data will include EEG signals under multiple experimental conditions, recording the participants' brain activity during the tasks.

Data Annotation: Each data segment has clear annotations, including decision time, choice type (gain/loss), and EEG waveform characteristics. Behavioral Data:

This includes records of each participant's choices during the experiment (e.g., the level of risk chosen, whether they accepted loss options, etc.) and reaction times.

The behavioral data will be analyzed in conjunction with EEG signals to explore the relationship between brain signals and behavioral responses.

Data Availability:

All data will be made publicly available on the GitHub platform for sharing and use by the academic community and researchers. The dataset includes raw EEG signals, preprocessed EEG data, experimental design files, behavioral data, and analysis scripts.

The data files include detailed README files and data usage instructions to ensure users can accurately understand and use the data.
